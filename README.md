\# RADAR



\*\*A decision-support prototype for identifying where management intervention is needed across a portfolio of interdependent initiatives.\*\*



\*\*Live Demo:\*\* https://kakalooka.github.io/RADAR_v2/

**5-minute walkthrough:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NXFyamm_zTc

\---



\## The Problem



Managing multiple initiatives across different teams becomes difficult when progress, dependencies and risks are reported in different formats and places.



A status may look acceptable in isolation while another blocked initiative is preventing it from moving forward. Missing or ambiguous data can further distort the overall picture.



RADAR was designed around one practical management question:



> \*\*Where should management step in and help?\*\*



The prototype uses a fully synthetic portfolio of 20 interconnected initiatives to explore how structured analysis and AI-assisted decision support can turn fragmented status information into a clearer intervention workflow.



\---



\## What RADAR Does



RADAR transforms portfolio data into an interactive management view that helps users:



\* visualize dependencies between initiatives,

\* identify blocked and at-risk work,

\* detect dependencies that may propagate delays,

\* prioritize initiatives requiring management attention,

\* surface data-quality issues before relying on the analysis,

\* inspect individual initiatives and recommended actions,

\* filter the portfolio by department, status and strategic group,

\* manage a working intervention queue,

\* generate an automatic portfolio summary by department.



The system does \*\*not\*\* make management decisions automatically. It identifies signals, structures the problem and suggests where attention may be needed — the final decision remains with the user.



\---



\## How the Analysis Works



RADAR deliberately combines structured decision logic with an AI-assisted prototyping approach.



Where an outcome can be calculated reliably from structured data — such as initiative status, dependencies, progress or missing information — the prototype uses deterministic logic.



These signals are then translated into an intervention-oriented workflow that distinguishes between different types and priorities of management attention.



For the supplied synthetic dataset, recommendation text is prepared and frozen in the prototype rather than generated by a runtime language model.



This keeps the prototype transparent and reproducible while demonstrating where an AI-generated recommendation layer could be introduced in a production version.



\---



\## Product Design Decisions



\### Intervention-first, not reporting-first



RADAR is designed primarily to answer \*\*where action is needed\*\*, rather than simply reproduce another portfolio status dashboard.



\### Dependencies matter



Initiatives are not evaluated only in isolation. Their relationships are visualized so that a problem in one part of the portfolio can be understood in the context of downstream work.



\### Data quality is part of the product



Missing or ambiguous information is surfaced explicitly because recommendations are only as reliable as the underlying portfolio data.



\### Human-in-the-loop



RADAR supports management judgement rather than replacing it. Recommendations can be reviewed and selected before they become part of the intervention workflow.

\## Prototype Architecture



RADAR is intentionally lightweight and self-contained.



\*\*Technology\*\*



\* HTML

\* CSS

\* JavaScript



\*\*Runtime\*\*



\* no backend,

\* no external API,

\* no installation,

\* no environment configuration,

\* runs directly in a browser.



A snapshot of the synthetic dataset is embedded in the prototype for immediate exploration.



RADAR also supports CSV import, allowing another portfolio dataset following the expected structure to be loaded directly in the browser.



\---



\## Prototype Limitations



RADAR is a proof-of-concept rather than a production system.



The current version:



\* operates on synthetic data,

\* has no persistent database,

\* has no authentication or role-based access control,

\* has no integrations with enterprise data sources,

\* does not use a runtime LLM or external AI API,

\* assumes a defined input-data structure,

\* uses prepared recommendation text for the demonstration dataset.



\---



\## From Prototype to Production



A production version could connect directly to project-management, CRM or reporting systems instead of relying on CSV input.



An AI/NLP layer could normalize unstructured status updates, extract risks and proposed actions, and generate contextual summaries dynamically. Deterministic rules would remain appropriate for signals that can be calculated reliably from structured data.



Production deployment would also require access controls, auditability, data-quality monitoring and a feedback loop allowing users to confirm or reject recommendations and improve the system over time.
